What is FIRE?
🔥 FIRE — Financial Independence, Retire Early
FIRE is a movement built around aggressive saving and investing so you can live off your portfolio returns — retiring decades earlier than the traditional age 65.

The core idea: if your portfolio is large enough that safe withdrawals cover your expenses forever, you're financially free.
📐 The 4% Rule
The 4% Rule (from the Trinity Study, 1998) states that withdrawing 4% of your portfolio per year has historically lasted 30+ years in 95%+ of market scenarios.

FIRE Number = Annual Expenses ÷ 0.04
Example: $60,000/yr expenses → $1,500,000 FIRE Number

Many lean FIRE adherents use 3.5% for extra safety. Fat FIRE often stays at 3%.
🌱 Lean FIRE
Living below the median household income in retirement — typically $25,000–$40,000/year.

Requires minimal lifestyle, geographic arbitrage (lower cost-of-living areas), or side income. Smallest FIRE number, fastest to reach.
Lean FIRE Number ≈ $750k – $1M
🔥 Regular FIRE
Maintaining a comfortable middle-class lifestyle in retirement — typically $40,000–$100,000/year.

The most common FIRE target. Assumes modest lifestyle without major sacrifices.
Regular FIRE Number ≈ $1M – $2.5M
💰 Fat FIRE
Retiring with $100,000+/year in spending — maintaining a luxurious lifestyle without financial compromise.

Usually requires high-income careers, business exits, or real estate. Takes significantly longer but provides maximum security.
Fat FIRE Number ≈ $2.5M – $5M+
🌊 Coast FIRE
The point at which you can stop contributing to investments and let compound growth alone carry you to your FIRE number by retirement age.

After reaching Coast FIRE, you only need to cover current living expenses — you can take lower-stress jobs or work part-time.
Coast Number = FIRE Number ÷ (1+r)^years
📊 Safe Withdrawal Rate (SWR)
The percentage of your portfolio you can withdraw annually without running out of money over a long retirement.

3% — Very safe, works in almost all historical scenarios including 60-year retirements
3.5% — Safe for 40–50 year retirements
4% — The classic rule. Safe for 30-year retirements
5%+ — Riskier, may deplete early in bad markets
⚡ Real Return vs Nominal Return
Nominal return: What you see on your brokerage statement (e.g., 7%)
Real return: Nominal minus inflation (e.g., 7% − 3% = 4%)

This calculator uses nominal returns in projections. Your actual purchasing power grows at the real rate. Always plan using real returns for expenses.
